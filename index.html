Research

My research addresses how language models can align with human values, not as a static, one-off training objective, but as something that is context-dependent and shaped through interaction. During my PhD, I developed methods to identify value-relevant signals in text (value inference); as a postdoc, I studied how language models can generate outputs that reflect different value orientations (value expression). In parallel, I work on subjectivity in NLP, modeling variation in interpretation rather than reducing it to a single ground truth.

I was recently awarded a Veni, a prestigious Dutch personal grant for early-career researchers. My project, Inference-Time Value Alignment in Multi-Turn Language Model Interaction, develops methods to infer user values from noisy, evolving signals during a conversation; to represent them in a form a language model can use; and to express them stably in generated language throughout a multi-turn exchange.
